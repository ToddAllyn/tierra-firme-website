The thesis

You're not broke. You're in the wrong market.

Across the US, Canada and Europe, the entry point to owning something has moved out of reach: a first home, a rental, a small business. That holds even for people with solid incomes who did everything right.

But "out of reach" is local. Whether you are stretching a fixed income into retirement or still earning and ready to build a first real asset, the same savings can buy more abroad. Three forces explain why, and each carries its own risk.

The framework

Three arbitrages, three risks.

Every gap that makes a market cheaper is also a reason to be careful. We teach both sides.

Questions investors ask

Sourced answers before you commit.

Short answers on ownership, registration, taxes, the exchange rate and visas, each with the primary sources.

Can foreigners buy property in Colombia?

Yes, with the same ownership rights as Colombians, if the money comes in through the formal exchange market and is registered.

Why do you have to register a foreign investment in Colombia?

Registration with Banco de la República is what protects your right to move the money and the sale proceeds back out.

What taxes do foreigners pay on property in Colombia?

Closing costs when you buy, annual predial while you own, tax on rent, and 15% capital gains tax when you sell after two years.

How does the exchange rate change your return on property abroad?

Your return is the local price change plus or minus the currency move. A strong local gain can disappear when converted home.
