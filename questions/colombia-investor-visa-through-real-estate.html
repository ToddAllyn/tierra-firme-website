Can buying property in Colombia get you a visa?

The Country · Research
By Todd Allyn, research lead, Tierra Firme · Updated October 23, 2026

Buying property can support Colombia's M (migrant) investor visa. Under Cancillería Resolution 5477 of 2022, the property must be in your name alone, worth at least 350 legal monthly minimum wages (SMMLV) measured at the date you apply, and registered as a foreign investment with Banco de la República. Meeting the amount does not guarantee approval; Cancillería decides each case.

What the file usually needs

Proof of ownership, the Banco de la República record showing the real estate investment in your name, a valid passport and the other documents Cancillería lists for the visa type. Check the current list on Cancillería's site before you apply.

From M visa to residency

After holding an investor M visa for the required time, a foreigner can apply for an R (resident) visa by accumulated time. Rules and amounts change, so confirm the current resolution.

Common mistakes

Buying jointly with a non-applicant, sending money informally so the investment is not registered, or measuring the amount with last year's minimum wage instead of the value on the application date.

More questions

Does the property have to be in my name only?

For the real estate route described by Castrillón y Cárdenas, yes: the property must be in the applicant's exclusive name.

How much is 350 SMMLV?

It depends on the minimum wage in force on your application date, which changes every year. Multiply the current SMMLV by 350.

Why do you have to register a foreign investment in Colombia?

Registration with Banco de la República is what protects your right to move the money and the sale proceeds back out.

Can foreigners buy property in Colombia?

Yes, with the same ownership rights as Colombians, if the money comes in through the formal exchange market and is registered.
