What are currency, political and structural arbitrage?

The Country · Research
By Todd Allyn, research lead, Tierra Firme · Updated October 23, 2026

They are three reasons the same savings can buy more in one country than another. Currency arbitrage comes from exchange rates. Political arbitrage comes from markets that price in more risk than exists. Structural arbitrage comes from rules on ownership, tax and repatriation. Each gap carries a matching risk, so the work is telling a real opportunity from a mispriced one.

Currency

A strong home currency buys more local assets. The same exchange rate can move against you while you hold, so the currency is part of the return, not a constant.

Political

Markets that headlines avoid can be priced below their real risk. Sometimes they are priced correctly, or too high. Research separates the two.

Structural

Ownership rules, taxes, registration and the ability to bring money home decide what you actually keep. These rules differ by country and change over time.

More questions

Is arbitrage risk-free?

No. In this sense it means a price gap between markets. Every gap has a reason, and the reason is the risk.

Which matters most?

It depends on the deal. For most buyers paying in dollars, currency has the largest effect on the final result.

How does the exchange rate change your return on property abroad?

Your return is the local price change plus or minus the currency move. A strong local gain can disappear when converted home.

How do you evaluate a country before investing there?

Information, evaluation, interpretation, then action: gather sourced facts, test them, read what they mean for you, and only then commit.

What taxes do foreigners pay on property in Colombia?

Closing costs when you buy, annual predial while you own, tax on rent, and 15% capital gains tax when you sell after two years.
