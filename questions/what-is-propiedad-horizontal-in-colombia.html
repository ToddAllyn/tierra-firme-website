What is propiedad horizontal in Colombia?

By Todd Allyn, research lead, Tierra Firme · Updated October 23, 2026

Propiedad horizontal is the legal regime, set by Law 675 of 2001, for apartment buildings and gated communities in Colombia. You own your unit outright and share ownership of the common areas with the other owners. Every owner pays a monthly administration fee (cuota de administración), and the building is run by an owners' assembly, an administrator and its own internal rules.

What the administration fee pays for

Security, cleaning, maintenance of shared areas, insurance and the building's reserve fund. Fees vary widely with amenities and size, and unpaid fees can be collected through legal action.

Rules that affect investors

The building's reglamento can limit uses such as short-term rentals, pets or renovations. Read it, and the last assembly minutes, before buying. They show disputes, planned special assessments and how well the building is run.

Questions to ask the administrator

Is the unit paid up? Are any special assessments (cuotas extraordinarias) approved or planned? How large is the reserve fund? Are short-term rentals allowed?

More questions

Is the administration fee optional?

No. It is an obligation of every owner under the building's regime and can be collected through the courts if unpaid.

Who decides big repairs?

The owners' assembly approves budgets and special assessments according to the building's rules and Law 675.

Can foreigners buy property in Colombia?

Yes, with the same ownership rights as Colombians, if the money comes in through the formal exchange market and is registered.

What taxes do foreigners pay on property in Colombia?

Closing costs when you buy, annual predial while you own, tax on rent, and 15% capital gains tax when you sell after two years.
