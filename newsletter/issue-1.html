The Asset · Commercial real estate · Murphy

The big buyers are already here

The largest Colombian deal on the Record this year is a property deal: the real estate fund Pei buying five office and logistics assets plus expansion land, about US$948 million. Mallplaza, part of the Falabella group, bought eight Gran Plaza shopping centers for about US$353 million.

Institutions study title, tenancy and operating costs before they price anything. A private buyer should do the same, starting with the basics: foreigners can own property in Colombia with the same rights as Colombians, and every building runs under its own propiedad horizontal rules.

The Capital · Business and family capital · Shawn

Plan the way out on the way in

Whether you buy an apartment or start a company, how your money arrives decides whether it can leave. Funds that come in through a Colombian bank or another authorized exchange intermediary are registered with Banco de la República as foreign investment, and that registration is what protects your right to send proceeds and profits home later.

Money that arrives informally can be hard to repatriate legally. For a family building something across two countries, this one step matters more than any single purchase.

The Country · Research · Todd

Your return has two currencies

If you earn in dollars, euros or loonies and own in pesos, your result is the local price change plus or minus the currency move. A strong gain in Colombia can shrink once it is converted home, and a weak peso on the day you buy can work in your favor.

That is why we read the country before the asset: where policy is heading, what the exchange rate is doing and what the rules let a foreigner take home. The deal flow above says serious capital is moving. The question for each of us is on what terms.
